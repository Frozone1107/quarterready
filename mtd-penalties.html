MTD penalties: what happens if a quarterly update is late?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax uses a points-based system for late submissions, instead of an automatic fine each time (GOV.UK).

Late submission: penalty points

  • Each missed deadline (a quarterly update or the tax return) earns one point. Only one point per deadline.
  • At 4 points you get a £200 penalty, and another £200 for each further missed deadline.
  • A single point expires after 24 months. Once at the threshold, you clear the points by meeting your deadlines for a period instead.
2026–27 concession: there are no penalties for missing a quarterly update deadline in the 2026–27 tax year. You still need to keep digital records and send the updates before your tax return (GOV.UK).

Late payment

Late payment penalties apply to tax paid late (not to payments on account). Interest runs from the first day. For 2026–27: nothing if paid within 15 days; 3% of the tax owed at day 15 if 16 to 30 days late (not in your first year); and if 31 days or more late, 3% at day 15, 3% at day 30, plus 10% a year on what's still owed (GOV.UK). In your first year you get 30 days before penalties start.

Volunteers

If you've signed up voluntarily, you don't get penalties for missed quarterly updates; the tax return threshold is 2 points instead of 4.

How to stay on time

  • Put the four dates (7 August, 7 November, 7 February, 7 May) in your calendar.
  • Record as you go, so each update is just copying totals.
  • You can send an update up to 10 days before the period ends if you expect no more transactions (GOV.UK).
