Can I use a spreadsheet for Making Tax Digital?

Yes. HMRC says bridging software may suit you if you want to keep your digital records in a spreadsheet (GOV.UK: find software). The spreadsheet holds your records; the bridging software reads the totals and sends them to HMRC.

How it works

  1. You record each income and expense in your spreadsheet (or an app that exports one).
  2. Each quarter, the spreadsheet adds up year-to-date totals per HMRC category.
  3. Bridging software reads those totals and submits your quarterly update.

What the spreadsheet needs

  • One row per transaction, with a date, a category and an amount.
  • Categories that match what HMRC asks for, for example "Rent, rates, insurance and ground rents" or "Car, van and travel expenses".
  • Year-to-date totals per category for each quarter, because each update covers from 6 April to the end of the quarter (GOV.UK).
  • Residential mortgage interest kept apart from other property expenses.

Finding bridging software

HMRC's software finder lists products that work with MTD for Income Tax, including bridging software that connects to spreadsheets (GOV.UK). Check that the one you pick supports the income sources you have (self-employment, UK property or both) and the file type you'll use, such as CSV or Excel.

Common questions

What is bridging software?

A program recognised by HMRC that takes figures from a spreadsheet or another tool and sends them to HMRC as your quarterly update. It doesn't keep your records itself.

Is there free bridging software?

Some products are free, often for simple affairs or a limited number of submissions. HMRC's software finder shows the options.

Can I send updates straight from Excel?

Not on its own. Excel holds the records; bridging software reads the totals and submits them.

Do I still need a tax return?

Yes. After the four updates, you finalise the year with a tax return from MTD-compatible software by 31 January.
