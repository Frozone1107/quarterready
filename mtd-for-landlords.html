Making Tax Digital for landlords: what changes and when

If you let out property in the UK and file a Self Assessment return, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax replaces the once-a-year paperwork with digital records and four short updates a year. Here is what it means for a landlord.

When does MTD start for landlords?

It depends on your qualifying income: your rental income plus any self-employment income, before expenses. Mortgage interest, repairs and agent fees don't reduce it.

Example: £24,000 of rent and £9,000 from a side business in 2025–26 gives £33,000, so MTD starts on 6 April 2027.

Jointly owned property

Only your share of the rent counts. If you and a sibling own a property 50/50 and it earns £50,000, your qualifying income from it is £25,000 (GOV.UK). More in MTD and jointly owned property.

Foreign property

If you're UK tax resident, rent from property abroad counts too. If you're not UK resident, only UK property income counts (GOV.UK).

What you'll do each quarter

Keep a digital record of each rent payment and each expense, then send HMRC a quarterly update: year-to-date totals for each category, not individual receipts (GOV.UK).

Property expenses go under HMRC's categories, for example "Rent, rates, insurance and ground rents", "Property repairs and maintenance" and "Legal, management and other professional fees". Residential mortgage interest is reported separately: it isn't deducted from profit but gives a basic-rate tax reduction.

If your property income is under £90,000, you can send one consolidated expenses figure instead of the breakdown.

Do I need one update per property?

No. All your UK let properties form one UK property business, so you send one combined update for them. Keeping a split by property is still useful for your own tracking and for your accountant.

What landlords should do now

  1. Add up your rent and other self-employment income for the year that decides your start date.
  2. Choose software, or a spreadsheet plus bridging software.
  3. Sign up before your start date.
  4. Start recording now, so your first update is just a matter of copying totals.
