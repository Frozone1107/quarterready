Working from home: HMRC's flat rate for the self-employed

If you're self-employed and work from home, you can claim a flat monthly amount instead of working out the business share of your bills (GOV.UK).

Hours of business work at home in the monthFlat rate for that month
25 to 50£10
51 to 100£18
101 or more£26

How it works

  • You need at least 25 hours of business work at home in a month to claim for that month.
  • The rate is set month by month, so a busy month and a quiet month can get different amounts.
  • It covers heating, electricity and similar household bills. Phone and internet aren't included: claim the business share of those separately.
  • Example: 60 hours a month all year is 12 × £18 = £216.

Flat rate or actual costs?

The flat rate is simple but small. If you use a room mainly for work, working out the business share of your actual bills can give more. You choose whichever suits you.

Where it goes in Making Tax Digital

Use-of-home costs go in the category "Rent, rates, power and insurance costs" in your quarterly update (see HMRC expense categories). In QuarterReady Records, add it as an expense in that category.
