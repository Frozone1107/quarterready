Making Tax Digital for sole traders and the self-employed

If you're self-employed (a sole trader, freelancer or contractor working for yourself) and file Self Assessment, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax changes how you keep records and report to HMRC.

When does it start for you?

HMRC looks at your qualifying income: your self-employment turnover plus any property income, before expenses (GOV.UK).

A salary from a job doesn't count, nor do pensions, dividends or a partnership profit share. See employed with a side business.

What changes

  • You keep a digital record of each sale and each business expense.
  • You send four quarterly updates a year: year-to-date totals per category, not individual invoices (GOV.UK).
  • You finish the year with a tax return from your software by 31 January.

The expense categories

MTD uses the same categories as the self-employment pages of the tax return: cost of goods, subcontractors, wages, car van and travel, rent rates power and insurance, repairs, phone and office costs, advertising, interest, bank charges, bad debts, professional fees, depreciation and other expenses. If your turnover is under £90,000 you can send one consolidated expenses figure instead.

Business mileage can be claimed at HMRC's flat rates: 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles from April 2026.
