HMRC expense categories for Making Tax Digital

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax uses the same expense categories as the Self Assessment tax return (GOV.UK). Each quarterly update sends a year-to-date total for each category, not your individual receipts.

Self-employment (sole traders)

CategoryExamples
Cost of goods bought for resale or goods usedStock, raw materials, parts
Construction industry: payments to subcontractorsCIS subcontractor payments
Wages, salaries and other staff costsEmployee pay, employer NI, pensions for staff
Car, van and travel expensesFuel, train tickets, hotels, or the 55p mileage rate
Rent, rates, power and insurance costsWorkshop rent, business rates, energy, insurance, use of home
Repairs and maintenance of property and equipmentFixing tools, servicing equipment
Phone, fax, stationery and other office costsPhone, internet, postage, software subscriptions
Advertising and business entertainment costsAds, website. Client entertaining isn't allowable.
Interest on bank and other loansBusiness loan interest
Bank, credit card and other financial chargesAccount fees, card processing fees
Irrecoverable debts written offInvoices you'll never be paid
Accountancy, legal and other professional feesAccountant, solicitor
Depreciation and loss or profit on sale of assetsNot allowable: claim capital allowances instead
Other business expensesAnything allowable that doesn't fit above

UK property (landlords)

CategoryExamples
Rent, rates, insurance and ground rentsLandlord insurance, ground rent, service charges, council tax on empty periods
Property repairs and maintenanceRepainting, fixing the boiler (not improvements)
Legal, management and other professional feesLetting agent, accountant, safety certificates
Costs of services provided, including wagesCleaning, gardening, utilities you pay for tenants
Travel costsTrips to the property for the letting business
Other allowable property expensesAdvertising for tenants, phone, stationery
Residential property finance costsMortgage interest. Kept separate: it gives a basic-rate tax reduction instead of reducing profit.

A shortcut under £90,000

If your turnover or property income is under £90,000, you can send one total for all expenses instead of the breakdown. See consolidated expenses. Residential finance costs still go separately.

In QuarterReady Records you pick the category when you add an expense (or import a bank statement), and it adds up each category for every quarterly update.
