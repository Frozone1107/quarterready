What counts

Self-employment turnover and property income, before expenses. For a jointly owned property, only your share. If you were UK resident, foreign property income counts too.

What doesn't

Employment (PAYE), pensions, dividends, and your profit share as a partner in a partnership. You may still need MTD for any self-employment or property income alongside them.

Spreadsheets are allowed

You can keep digital records in a spreadsheet, as long as you send quarterly updates through HMRC-recognised bridging software that connects to it.

Questions

Will HMRC tell me if I need to start?

HMRC reviews your Self Assessment return and writes to you if your qualifying income is over the threshold. If no letter arrives, it is still your responsibility to check and sign up.

Which year's income decides my start date?

Over £50,000 in 2024–25 means 6 April 2026. Over £30,000 in 2025–26 means 6 April 2027. Over £20,000 in 2026–27 means 6 April 2028, according to current GOV.UK guidance.

What if I'm under £20,000?

You're automatically exempt while your qualifying income stays at £20,000 or less. Check again each year, because your start date depends on each year's figures.

Do quarterly updates replace my tax return?

No. Quarterly updates are summaries of income and expenses. You still finalise the year with a tax return, sent from your MTD software, by 31 January after the tax year ends.

What happens after I buy?

Our payment partner emails you a receipt with your licence key straight away. Open the app, go to Settings, paste the key and press Activate. If anything goes wrong, see the help page.

Where are my records stored?

Only in your browser, on your device. We never receive them. Download a backup from Settings regularly, and keep it with your business records, because clearing your browser data deletes them.

Is this checker official?

No. QuarterReady is independent and not affiliated with HMRC. It applies the published thresholds to the figures you enter. Your situation may involve rules it doesn't cover, such as exemptions, non-UK residence or accounting periods that aren't 12 months.
