The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April and is split into four quarters. Each quarter's update is due about a month after the quarter ends.

How does it work?

Every three months, you send HMRC a summary of your income and expenses. QuarterReady does the first two steps for you.

  1. In QuarterReady

    Record

    Type in each income and expense, or import your bank statement and tick the business lines. Attach receipts and log business miles.

  2. In QuarterReady

    Add up

    Every line goes under the right HMRC category. You see year-to-date totals for each quarter, ready to send, with warnings for duplicates.

  3. In HMRC-recognised bridging software

    Send

    Copy in the totals or upload the CSV file. The bridging software submits them to HMRC. HMRC lists free options for simple affairs.

      Questions

      What counts as income for MTD?

      Your self-employment turnover and your rental income, before expenses, your share only. Wages (PAYE), pensions, dividends and a partner's share of partnership profit don't count.

      Why do I need another tool to send my updates?

      Only software on HMRC's recognised list may connect to its systems. QuarterReady is independent, so it prepares your figures; you then copy them into HMRC-recognised bridging software, which sends them. HMRC lists free options for simple affairs.

      What do the words mean?

      MTD: keeping digital records and sending a summary every three months. Qualifying income: turnover plus rent, before expenses. Tax year: 6 April to 5 April. Quarterly update: your income and expenses so far this tax year, by category. Bridging software: an HMRC-recognised program that sends figures prepared elsewhere. Tax return: still due by 31 January after the tax year.

      Will HMRC tell me if I need to start?

      HMRC reviews your Self Assessment return and writes to you if your qualifying income is over the threshold. If no letter arrives, it's still your responsibility to check and sign up.

      Does QuarterReady send my updates to HMRC?

      No. QuarterReady prepares the figures. To send them, use HMRC-recognised bridging software: copy in the totals or upload the CSV export, and it submits them for you.

      How do you keep up with rule changes?

      We check HMRC's guidance every month and after each Budget, and update the thresholds, deadlines and rates in the checker and the app. The date of the last check is shown under "What's changed".

      Do quarterly updates replace my tax return?

      No. Quarterly updates are summaries of income and expenses. You still finish the year with a tax return from MTD software by 31 January after the tax year ends.

      Where are my records stored?

      Only in your browser, on your device. We never receive them. Download a backup from Settings regularly, because clearing your browser data deletes them.

      Can I put it on my phone like an app?

      Yes. Open the app, then use "Add to Home Screen" (iPhone, in Safari) or "Install app" (Android and computer, in Chrome or Edge). It gets its own icon, opens with its own start screen and works offline. On Android and computers, a long press (or right-click) on the icon gives shortcuts: add a record, quarterly totals, the full version and check for updates.

      What happens after I buy?

      Our payment partner emails you a receipt with your licence key straight away. The full version unlocks by itself straight after payment; the licence key in your receipt is a backup. Your plan renews each year; you can cancel any time from the link in your receipt, and the full version stays on until the end of the year you paid for. The help page covers anything that goes wrong.

      What if I cancel?

      Cancel any time from the link in your receipt email. The full version stays on until the end of the year you paid for, then the app goes back to the free version. Your records stay on your device, and you can still view, back up and export what you need.

      Is this official?

      No. QuarterReady is independent and not affiliated with HMRC. It applies published rules to the figures you enter; your situation may involve rules it doesn't cover.
