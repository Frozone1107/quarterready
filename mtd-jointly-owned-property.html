MTD and jointly owned property: does the whole rent count?

No. Only your share counts. For Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, your qualifying income from a jointly owned property is your share of the income, before expenses (GOV.UK: work out your qualifying income).

HMRC's example

You own a property with your sibling. It brings in £50,000 a year and you each get an equal share. You have no self-employment income. Your qualifying income is £25,000, not £50,000.

If you only learn your share after expenses have been taken off (for example from a managing agent), HMRC uses that figure.

Add it to your other income

Your share is added to any self-employment turnover and any other property income. Over £30,000 in 2025–26 means MTD from 6 April 2027; over £20,000 in 2026–27 means 6 April 2028.

Half of a jointly let flat (£24,000 rent)£12,000

What each owner does

Each joint owner who is over the threshold keeps their own digital records for their share and sends their own quarterly updates. In QuarterReady Records, each owner records their own share.
